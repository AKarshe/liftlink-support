LiftLink Support Privacy

Privacy Policy

Effective October 7, 2026

LiftLink ("the app") lets you view and control garage doors you've added to Apple Home. This policy explains what information the app uses and what happens to it. In short: LiftLink doesn't collect, store on any server, sell, or share your personal data.

Information the app doesn't collect

LiftLink has no user accounts, no servers of its own, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party SDKs. It doesn't track you across apps or websites, and it doesn't send any information about you, your home, or your garage to the developer or anyone else.

Apple Home (HomeKit) data

With your permission, LiftLink uses Apple's HomeKit framework to read and change the state of the garage door openers in your Apple Home: home names, garage and room names, whether a door is open or closed, obstruction and jam status, door position, and whether an accessory is reachable.

  • This data is read directly from Apple Home on your device and is used only to show door status and send the open or close commands you request.
  • HomeKit data is never transmitted to the developer, never shared with third parties, and never used for advertising, marketing, or data mining.
  • You can withdraw access at any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > HomeKit.

Face ID, Touch ID, and passcode

LiftLink asks iOS to confirm it's you before opening a garage (and, if you choose, before closing one). Authentication is handled entirely by iOS. The app only receives a yes-or-no result; it never has access to your face data, fingerprint, or passcode.

Information stored on your device

LiftLink keeps the following on your device so it works between launches:

  • Settings: the Home you selected, any nicknames you give your garages, appearance choices, reminder and announcement preferences, whether closing requires authentication, and whether you've completed setup.
  • Activity history: a list of up to 200 recent garage events (for example, "Door 1 closed at 6:02 PM"), shown on the Activity tab.
  • Widget data: your garage names and their latest status, shared with LiftLink's own widgets so they can show your doors.
  • Reminders: if you turn on open-door reminders, they're scheduled as notifications on your device.

None of this leaves your device, except as part of your own device backups (for example, iCloud Backup), which Apple protects under its own privacy policy. Deleting the app removes it.

Diagnostics

The app writes basic technical messages (for example, "garage command failed") to your device's system log to help diagnose problems. These stay on your device and aren't sent to the developer. If you choose to share analytics or crash reports with app developers in iOS Settings, Apple may provide anonymized crash reports to the developer, as described in Apple's privacy policy.

Beta testing through TestFlight

If you test a pre-release version through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share certain information with the developer, such as the email address or name you were invited with, your device model, and any feedback, screenshots, or crash reports you choose to submit. This is handled by Apple under the Apple Privacy Policy.

Children's privacy

LiftLink doesn't knowingly collect any personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
